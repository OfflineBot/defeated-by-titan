# Inspiration and reference

## The source material: Attack on Titan

The fantasy comes straight from the manga/anime: humans against giants, omni-directional
mobility gear, the nape as the one weak spot, walled cities. If a player knows the show,
the game should feel immediately familiar: hooks into buildings, gas-powered swings, dual
blades, the dive onto the neck.

What we take from it:

- the gear fantasy (hooks, gas, paired blades) and the nape kill
- walled rings as the shape of the world
- the scale contrast: a small fast human against slow enormous bodies
- the dread of open ground and the safety of height

What we deliberately change:

- The war is lost. The source material is about defending and reclaiming; our world
  already fell. That removes the military-campaign framing and replaces it with salvage
  work in a dead city. Quieter, sadder, and it explains why so few people are out there.
- No named cast, no story retelling. It's the world, not the plot.
- The weak spot is not painted on. In the show everyone knows where to cut; in the game
  that knowledge is the player's, not the UI's.

## The benchmark: Attack on Titan Revolution (Roblox)

AoT Revolution is the best playable version of this fantasy right now, so it's our
measuring stick, not our template. The concrete bar: a blind test, ten testers, our
movement has to score at least even with it.

Where we intend to beat it:

- physics-honest swinging: momentum is real and conserved, a good arc carries you,
  nothing teleports or cheats
- everything is hookable - no gamey whitelist of anchor points
- the rope is a hard physical constraint (it never stretches), which makes swings
  predictable and learnable
- honest hitboxes: what you see is what you can hit and hook, everywhere
- readable speed: the picture stays legible at full velocity instead of dissolving
  into effects

Where Revolution is allowed to stay ahead for now: content volume, polish of animations,
sound. Those come after the movement bar is met.
