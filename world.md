# World

One playable district for now: Ashgate, the fallen outer ring.

## The city

Timber-frame quarters, narrow lanes, a dense varied roofscape. A monumental outer wall
with two gates (one heavy main gate, one lighter). A river with bridges and bank towers.
Ruin fields and rubble where the war went through. A garrison building you can enter -
it's the hub.

The city reads abandoned, not destroyed to gravel: structurally intact enough to swing
through, but nobody lives here. No market bustle, no lit lanterns, carts left where they
stopped. Decay and overgrowth instead of gore.

## Terrain

The ground must be dramatic: hills, terraces, real relief. Flat ground was built, played
and rejected. The district itself should sit ON the landform - streets that climb,
quarters at different heights - not on a leveled plate with scenery hills outside.

Height is traversal fuel. This is the one world-design law that is not negotiable: rope
movement dies without tall things at the right spacing. Every wall ledge, tower, ridge
and cliff face is a rung in a ladder the player climbs with momentum. A district was once
measured where the roofscape alone couldn't carry a fast route - the fix is architecture
(towers, spires, wall works), never "swing lower."

## Water

Swimmable. Being in water slows you and burns gas faster. Hooks don't attach to water -
a shot into the river is a miss.

## Edges

No visible world edge. An invisible boundary turns you back, and if a bug ever drops you
through the floor you're teleported back to safe ground. Falling out of the world is
never a death.
