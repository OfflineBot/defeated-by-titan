# Concept

Third-person 3D action game, low poly. You're a soldier with the vector gear: two
grappling hooks, two gas tanks, two blades. The city belongs to the titans now. You swing
through it, and the only way to bring a titan down is a fast cut through the cortex at
the back of its neck.

The game is movement first. Combat exists to test how well you move. If swinging through
the city doesn't feel great on its own, nothing else matters.

## Setting in short

Humanity holds three concentric fortress rings: Ashgate (outer), Ironrose (middle),
Highspire (inner). Our twist: the war is already lost. Ashgate fell decades ago. Missions
are salvage runs into your own ruins. Nobody is taking the city back, and the game never
pretends otherwise.

Tone: quiet, melancholic. Not horror. An empty city under an ash sky. The palette is
muted and desaturated, which makes warm color mean something - a lamp, a fire, a titan's
skin. Three signal colors are reserved for gameplay and appear nowhere else:

- cyan: a rope will hold here
- amber: the cortex
- crimson: danger

## Priorities

1. Movement before everything. No skill tree, no economy, no raids, no cosmetics until
   the swinging convinces by itself.
2. The measurable bar: a blind test against Attack on Titan Revolution with ten testers.
   Our movement has to score at least even. See inspiration.md.
3. Readability at speed. Every visual decision gets judged while swinging at 60 m/s:
   can you still read titan silhouettes, the amber point, the structure of the city.
